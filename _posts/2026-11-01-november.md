## Nov 1

Accessibility took the day: 98 pages pass an automated check, and twenty apps got fixes. Sighting and Co-Stanza went to App Review; Apple wants every post reportable and blockable. Joshua Tree ran full screen on a real Raspberry Pi 4 for the first time. Board testing found five bugs, all fixed that afternoon; release eight passed.

## Nov 2

Joshua Tree shipped Shortcuts, Bluetooth and YouTube; its Pi graphics chip runs on a driver I wrote myself.

Apple rejected Keyrate as spam, matching its word list to two typing tests. I rebuilt the list from public-domain novels; both are back in review.

Apple rejected Elah, Co-Stanza and Sighting as spam lookalikes.
After comparing ten rival browsers, I added downloads, site icons, a fire button and translation. Mac is in review, iPhone waits.

## Nov 14

## Nov 15

Joshua Tree's dimming and saver are merged. The Pi's auto lock is in review: only my password opens it.

I built the three pieces Doorstock's spec was missing: spring costs, a label scanner and a monthly accountant CSV. Mac is built; iPhone waits on the appeal.

Talli got a money log with a yearly limit bar; web and server are live.

Kernel now imports a PDF or EPUB you own on every device and summarizes each chapter. Then the library came up empty; I recovered most summaries from cache.

Epiphany is free on the web now; Pro is $1.99 a month or $14.99 a year, and anyone who paid $1 keeps it for life. I shut three billing holes; the iPhone paywall is next. I killed Gato and dropped the CanLII app; Temida does that search.

## Nov 16

Board night ran past midnight on a stale card with the wrong clock; every secure download failed. Paddle froze because every frame repainted the whole screen; now it repaints only what moved. I also started a carrier board for the Compute Module 5.

Joshua Tree's version 14 was meant for printing, but I have no printer, so it shipped as graphics acceleration; the Mac now drives the Pi's screen. I also started a 64-bit PC port, which boots in the emulator.
Samantha writes captions by listening; the first test was word for word. Her trailer is done: thirty seconds, narrated. Her training runs alone, with safer precision after a math overflow. The LaCie is gone, taking the 9B model's data with it.

All 58 Apple Notes went into 26 projects' roadmaps, private bits into my vault; Notes is empty.

## Nov 17

Keyrate got a new icon and a drill mode built from the keys I miss most; both builds wait for review.

Samantha's video tools are finished: compress a clip, grab a still, lay a song under a video. The landing opens on her trailer behind the headline; scrolling no longer kills the live iPhone demo.

## Nov 18

The landing lost its typewriter headline for good; a build check blocks it. A new desktop section shows Spaces, Mission Control, Spotlight and Launchpad.
I graded the Joshua Tree landing a B-: good content, muddled order, too many repeats. I cut the repeats and made the desktop clips one swipeable strip.Turing's picker reached its major release. A fresh sealed set of 420 rows passed all three bars; a new guard refuses about half as many right answers. It's on Hugging Face; both sealed sets are spent.

I freed 2.5 GB tidying the code folder, and repaired my Claude skills after a Codex find-and-replace mangled them; all 29 link to one folder.

Vancouver Vice's Unreal project lived only on the LaCie, so the city and builds are gone. The web game and map data survived on GitHub; I'm rebuilding from those.

## Nov 21

Airwell passed App Review and went live. Nimble's landing types a question and lands the answer huge behind the headline; I fixed two API bugs: cancels errored, and failures got cached.

Joshua Tree is at 202 open issues, aiming for under 200; I reopened three I closed too early. Filters ruined the desert wallpaper, but 0.25 Higgsfield credits in GPT Image 2.5 made a real wax-crayon painting. Joshua graded it A, the house style; a true 16:9 cut, a quiet dock and a 16-plus colour kernel format are still open.

A security audit across every app with a server found four real holes. Epiphany's CDN cached one person's private data by URL, so anyone could read it for an hour; fixed, and GitHub sign-in now needs a verified email. Password reset links in jaybulb-api could open a custom app link, so one tap could take over an account; now they open only our https pages. Talli let any signed-in user read other people's files, and Melete could be tricked into linking a squatted signup. All four shipped. Turing's public voice endpoint still lets anyone make his cloned voice say any text; locking it waits on my call.

Apple's check said Epiphany's iPhone build had ads; it has none, so I resubmitted. Conics bounced on a name mismatch; fixed. Sighting's review notes now cover account deletion; it's back in. Mailbag's Gmail sign-in got stuck on Google's unverified-app page, so it takes a Google app password. Scrounger was rejected for user posts, so it got a terms screen, Report and Block; Apple may want a screen recording on a real phone, that's on me. Elah was rejected twice for spam; I resubmitted with a cross-reference map and a new icon.

I read 43 saved X posts in full, filed them in my vault, then cleared them off X. The corpus graded B: strong on tools, thin on original ideas. Melete's daily idea starts from those posts, one a day from tomorrow. Its landing now uses the house kit.

## Nov 22

The Connect Mouse shortcut froze for fifteen seconds at a time, so I rebuilt it as Tether, a menu bar app that brings a Bluetooth mouse back from the keyboard. A code review found ten problems and I fixed eight. The icon is now a blue Liquid Glass mouse, and the brand went blue.
