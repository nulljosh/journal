## Nov 1

Accessibility took the day: 98 pages pass an automated check; twenty apps got fixes.

Sighting and Co-Stanza went to App Review; Apple wants every post reportable and blockable. Epiphany dropped its growth calculator and takes budget income from paydays.
Joshua Tree ran full screen on a real Raspberry Pi 4. Board testing found five bugs, fixed that afternoon; release eight passed with Bluetooth.

## Nov 2

Duke ran Papers, Please overnight; it reached day 14 and learned to defuse the bomb.

Joshemon, my Game Boy game, went public, then got six releases.

Joshua Tree shipped Shortcuts, Bluetooth and YouTube; its Pi graphics chip runs on a driver I wrote by hand.
Keyrate got its App Store record, free in 175 countries and collecting nothing. Vancouver Vice built its first Mac app, with our own typeface, Seawall.

## Nov 6

I filed 31 Apple Notes into roadmaps and the vault. I added eleven Kernel chapters from four books and deleted 184 source photos; 566 still wait.

Apple rejected Keyrate as spam, matching its word list to two typing tests. I rebuilt the list from public domain novels; both are back in review.

## Nov 7

Cyllene is back before Apple with a reviewer key and demo video; twelve apps wait. Nine projects broke under Xcode 27; all are fixed.

Apple rejected Elah, Co-Stanza and Sighting as spam lookalikes. App-specific replies to all six threads got Horula and Atlin through.

After comparing ten rival browsers, I added downloads, site icons, a fire button, HTTPS first and translation. Mac is in review, iPhone waits.

## Nov 8

iPhone and Mac builds are back in review. Plaintxt's builds wait; Apple wants bigger iOS changes or I drop iOS.

Cyllene's onboarding has four real screens; real login ships after iPhone review.

## Nov 9

The iPhone and Mac update is in review; the web is live.
Joshua Tree's next build merged, closing 32 issues; PC ping is open.

Epiphany's iPhone statement upload never worked, so my October statement missed the graph; the fix is untested.

## Nov 10

Hamurapi's dark landing shows the game at night; its trailer is approved on both platforms.

Apple rejected Patois for not signing in with Apple, which was never spam. The sheet stayed open after sign-in, so it looked dead. Fixed; the iPhone build is back in review.

## Nov 11

Typed math answers count in any order, and math courses show x², √2 and π. Pre-Calc 11 went from ten to sixty questions; Pre-Calc 12 explains every answer.

I built a Things page for jaybulb.com: 55 things I use. Patois still said Tonchi in password reset emails; I fixed that and swept out old names.

## Nov 13

Apple locked my developer account until I accepted a new agreement. Tocks was rejected for background audio; sleep sounds play on a locked phone, so I asked for a fix. Apple asked what Delete account does; lost-pet posts now go too. Pan became Conics; Apple says it isn't ready. Co-Stanza is live on iPhone and Mac.

## Nov 14

Sent an iPhone update to Apple. The one-word Nimble name belongs to another developer.

## Nov 15

I spent the morning making Nimble faster.

Joshua Tree's dimming and saver are merged. The Pi's auto lock is in review: only my password opens it. Network printing is written.

I checked Doorstock against its spec and built the three missing pieces: spring costs, a label scanner and a monthly accountant CSV. Mac is built; iPhone waits on the appeal.

Echo added Word export everywhere and moved its Mac hot key.

Talli got a money log for earnings and expenses, with a yearly limit bar feeding the tax export. The web and server are live; iPhone and Mac need TestFlight.

Kernel now imports a PDF or EPUB you own on every device and summarizes each chapter. Then my Kernel library came up empty, with the summaries gone after November 10; I got most back from an old Mac cache and a database copy.

Epiphany is free on the web now; Pro is $1.99 a month or $14.99 a year, and anyone who paid $1 keeps it for life. I shut three billing holes, testing each live; the iPhone paywall is next. I killed Gato and dropped the CanLII app; Temida does that search.

## Nov 16

Board night ran past midnight on a stale card that could not set its clock, so every secure download failed. Paddle froze because every frame repainted the whole screen; now it paints only what moved, with a ball trail and a big score. The card still needs one reflash. I also started a carrier board for the Compute Module 5, with a blueprint and a mockup.

Joshua Tree's version 14 was going to be printing and scanning; it shipped: notes print to a network printer, the Pi finds printers, and a scan pulls a page into Photos. I have no printer, so 14 became graphics acceleration, and its first job cleared a test tile on the board. The Mac can now drive the Pi and see its screen. A blue default with an accent choice, and the chip's paint and copy commands, are in review. I also started the 64-bit PC port, the road to 15: a 64-bit kernel boots in the emulator, not pushed yet. The Pi never freed the memory its jobs used, so the third scan ran out; now each job frees it. Wi-Fi stopped dropping packets. The Mac Mini's 4B coding model now writes and fixes code on its own, slow but working.

Samantha writes captions by listening now: with no subtitle file beside a video, she transcribes it with Whisper; the first real test came out word for word. Her trailer is done: thirty seconds, thirteen things she did on this Mac, narrated. Filming turned up two bugs, both fixed. The landing now shows her working behind the headline. Her training runs without me; the first round scored zero because the math overflowed to NaN, so it now runs in safer precision and stops at the first NaN. The LaCie drive is gone, so the 9B model and that data are lost until I get a new drive.

Then I cleared the backlog. The notes routine now reads every account, keeps links, and deletes a note only after filing it. All 58 Apple Notes went into the roadmaps of 26 projects, private bits into my vault, and Notes is empty now. Apple's new AI compliance forms came in by mail; I will likely drop mainland China from the AI apps.

## Nov 17

Keyrate moved into its own repo with a new icon, a blue brand and a drill mode that builds practice from the keys I miss most. Apple had a broad outage today, sign-in and App Store Connect included, so I could not reach the iPhone review thread to clear a stale draft. Both Keyrate builds still wait for review from Nov 6; nothing was rejected. Turing shipped three releases, one that compresses video and one that grabs a still frame.
