## Nov 1

Accessibility took the day: 98 pages pass an automated check; twenty apps got fixes. Sighting and Co-Stanza went to App Review; Apple wants every post reportable and blockable. Joshua Tree first ran full screen on a real Raspberry Pi 4; board testing found five bugs, fixed by afternoon, and release eight passed.

## Nov 2

Joshua Tree shipped Shortcuts, Bluetooth and YouTube; its Pi graphics chip runs on a driver I wrote.

## Nov 6

I filed 31 Apple Notes, added eleven Kernel chapters from four books, and deleted 184 source photos; 566 wait.

Apple rejected Keyrate as spam over two typing tests. I rebuilt its word list from public-domain novels; both are back in review.

## Nov 7

Nine projects broke under Xcode 27; all are fixed.

Apple rejected Elah, Co-Stanza and Sighting as spam lookalikes. After comparing ten rival browsers, I added downloads, site icons, a fire button and translation. Mac is in review, iPhone waits.

## Nov 9

The iPhone and Mac update is in review; the web is live.

## Nov 10

Hamurapi's trailer is approved everywhere.

Apple rejected Patois for not signing in with Apple, which was never spam. Fixed; the iPhone build is back in review.

## Nov 11

Typed math answers count in any order, and math courses show x², √2 and π. Pre-Calc 11 went from ten to sixty questions; Pre-Calc 12 explains every answer.

## Nov 13

Apple locked my developer account until I accepted a new agreement. Tocks was rejected for background audio, and Apple asked what Delete account does; lost-pet posts go too. Co-Stanza is live on iPhone and Mac.

## Nov 14

Sent an iPhone update to Apple. The Nimble name belongs to another developer.

## Nov 15

The Pi's auto lock is in review: only my password opens it.

I built the three pieces Doorstock's spec was missing: spring costs, a label scanner and a monthly accountant CSV. Mac is built; iPhone waits on the appeal. Talli got a money log with a yearly limit bar; web and server are live.

Kernel imports a PDF or EPUB you own and summarizes each chapter.
Epiphany is free on the web; Pro is $1.99 a month or $14.99 a year, and anyone who paid $1 keeps it for life. I shut three billing holes; the iPhone paywall is next. I killed Gato and the CanLII app, since Temida does that search.

## Nov 16

Board night ran past midnight; a stale card with the wrong clock broke every secure download. Paddle froze because every frame repainted the whole screen; now it repaints only what moved. I started a carrier board for the Compute Module 5.

Joshua Tree's version 14 was meant for printing, but it shipped as graphics acceleration; the Mac now drives it. I started a 64-bit PC port. Samantha's captioner writes captions by listening; the first test was word for word. Her trailer is done. The LaCie is gone, taking the 9B model's data with it.

All 58 Apple Notes went into 26 projects' roadmaps, private bits into my vault; Notes is empty.

## Nov 17

Keyrate got a new icon and a drill mode built from the keys I miss most; both are in review.

Samantha's video tools are finished. The landing opens on her trailer; scrolling no longer kills the live iPhone demo.

## Nov 18

The landing lost its typewriter headline. A new desktop section shows Spaces, Mission Control, Spotlight and Launchpad.

I graded the Joshua Tree landing a B-, cut its repeats and made the desktop clips one swipeable strip. Turing's picker reached its major release. A fresh sealed set of 420 rows passed all three bars; a new guard refuses about half as many right answers. Both sealed sets are spent.

I freed 2.5 GB and fixed my Claude skills after a Codex find-and-replace mangled them.

Vancouver Vice's Unreal project lived only on the LaCie, so the city and builds are gone. I'm rebuilding from the web game and map data on GitHub.

## Nov 21

Airwell passed App Review and went live. I fixed two API bugs: cancels errored and failures got cached.

Filters ruined the desert wallpaper, so 0.25 credits bought a real wax-crayon painting. Joshua graded it A; open: a true 16:9 cut and a quiet dock.

A security audit of every server app found four real holes. Epiphany's CDN cached one person's private data by URL, readable by anyone for an hour; fixed. Password reset links could open any app link, so one tap could take over an account; now only https opens. Talli let any signed-in user read others' files, and Melete could be tricked into linking a squatted signup. All four shipped. Turing's public voice endpoint still lets anyone make his cloned voice say any text; locking it waits on me.

Apple said Epiphany's iPhone build had ads; it has none, so I resubmitted. Sighting's notes now cover account deletion, and it's back in. Scrounger was rejected for user posts; it got a terms screen with Report and Block. Elah was rejected twice for spam; I resubmitted with a new map and icon.

I read 43 saved X posts and cleared them off X. They graded B, strong on tools and thin on original ideas, and now seed Melete's daily idea.

## Nov 22

The Connect Mouse shortcut froze for fifteen seconds at a time, so I rebuilt it as Tether, a menu bar app that revives a Bluetooth mouse. A review found ten problems; I fixed eight. Hormuz got the Keyrate look; the strait is still shut on day 268.

## Nov 23

I shipped Echo's new release with Word export and a Mac hotkey for dictation, and rebuilt its landing around the live view. Patois got its parrot icon back, and I redrew the 32 Things icons as metal, glass and wood. Checking my saved X ideas against the apps, Echo's keyboard dictation was already in 3.0, and the simulator trimmer and Tripwire caching were not worth building; I also mapped the bookmarks into my vault as a graph. Apple's on-device model also runs on Private Cloud Compute with a window four times bigger; I asked Apple for access today, and Nimble, Echo, Cyllene and Plaintxt get it once it lands.

I sorted 87 Apple Notes into 25 roadmaps, live bugs first, then emptied Notes. A pull wiped my terminal settings; I restored them from GitHub and stopped tracking them. Two skills are still out.

Kernel got a big batch of books. Apple's built-in reader took 477 photographed pages in a minute. Hackers and Painters is whole now, Claude AI for Dummies has chapters 4 to 16, Video Game Design for Dummies has 13 and 14, and Einstein and Relativity is a new book. Plank got its book. I rewrote the guide in No Starch style: a big chapter number and a Bruegel painting at each opening, and a Try It Yourself box ending each chapter. The first pass added seven drawn figures and a summary per chapter; the PDF is live.

Listing photos in Scrounger had gone grey on iPhone and Mac, and city names read wrong; both are fixed.
