## Nov 1

Accessibility took the day: 98 pages pass an automated check and twenty apps got fixes. Sighting and Co-Stanza went to App Review; Apple wants every post reportable and blockable. Joshua Tree first ran full screen on a Pi 4; board testing found five bugs, all fixed.

## Nov 2

Joshua Tree shipped Shortcuts, Bluetooth and YouTube; its Pi graphics chip runs on a driver I wrote.

## Nov 6

I filed 31 Apple Notes, added eleven Kernel chapters, and deleted 184 source photos; 566 wait.

Apple rejected Keyrate as spam; I rebuilt its word list from public-domain novels; it's back in review.

## Nov 7

Nine projects broke under Xcode 27; all fixed.

Apple rejected Elah, Co-Stanza and Sighting as spam lookalikes. I added downloads, site icons, a fire button and translation. Mac is in review, iPhone waits.

## Nov 10

Apple rejected Patois for not signing in with Apple, which was never spam. Fixed; the iPhone build is back in review.

## Nov 13

Apple locked my account until I accepted a new agreement. Tocks was rejected for background audio; Apple asked what Delete account does.

## Nov 15

The Pi's auto lock is in review: only my password opens it.

I built the three pieces Doorstock's spec lacked: spring costs, a label scanner and an accountant CSV. Mac is built; iPhone waits on appeal.
Epiphany is free on the web; Pro is $1.99 a month or $14.99 a year, and $1 buyers keep it for life. I shut three billing holes; the iPhone paywall is next.

## Nov 16

A stale card clock broke secure downloads. Paddle froze from repainting every frame; now only moving parts repaint.

Joshua Tree shipped graphics acceleration; the Mac now drives it. I started a 64-bit PC port.

## Nov 18

A new desktop section shows Spaces, Mission Control, Spotlight and Launchpad.

I graded the Joshua Tree landing a B-, cut its repeats and made the desktop clips one swipe.

Vancouver Vice's Unreal project was lost with the LaCie; I'm rebuilding it from the web game's data.

## Nov 21

Airwell passed App Review and went live. I fixed two API bugs: cancels errored and failures got cached.

A security audit of every server app found four holes, all shipped: Epiphany's CDN cached one person's data for an hour, a reset link could take over an account, Talli let users read others' files, and Melete could link a squatted signup. Turing's public voice endpoint still lets anyone make his cloned voice say any text; I'm locking it.

Apple said Epiphany's iPhone build had ads; it has none, so I resubmitted. Scrounger was rejected for user posts; it got a terms screen with Report and Block. Elah was rejected twice for spam; I resubmitted with a new map and icon.

I cleared 43 saved X posts; they graded B and seed Melete's idea.

## Nov 22

The Connect Mouse shortcut froze for fifteen seconds, so I rebuilt it as Tether, a menu bar app that revives a mouse. Hormuz got the Keyrate look; the strait is still shut, day 268.

## Nov 23

I shipped Echo's release with Word export and a Mac dictation hotkey, and rebuilt its landing around the live view. Patois got its parrot icon back, and I redrew the 32 Things icons. I asked Apple for its bigger cloud model; four apps get it once it lands.

I sorted 87 Apple Notes into 25 roadmaps and emptied Notes.
Scrounger's grey photos and wrong city names are fixed.

## Nov 25

Almost nobody was buying, so nine small apps went free as a test, leaving Echo, Kernel and Epiphany alone. On December 23 I'll check whether price or discovery was the problem.

Airwell got the biggest push: themes, a welcome screen and eleven languages, checked in German and Arabic at max text size. The update is with Apple; its Product Hunt launch waits.

I cleaned up App Store Connect: deleted Oohaah and Sidewise, and dropped China from all 21 live apps to kill Apple's China banners. Apple's reasons: Elah and Cartons are spam, Mailbag and Scrounger got 2.1 issues.

Its Get Pro button is with Apple; once approved, the app goes free and sells Pro on the web.
## Nov 26

My Bluetooth mouse dropped overnight and Tether couldn't revive it, since it came back at a new address. I re-paired it by hand and taught Tether to do that after 15 seconds, still untested on a real drop. It also checks the device list now, and ships as a notarized release on GitHub.

I cut a dead uptime check. Blackcomb's next version is built and uploaded, with the welcome screen, start page, tab overview and translate, but not sent to Apple; its Mac build's automation waits on my yes. The landing snows only when it's really snowing in Whistler, and I cut a 9:16 ad from the real app.

My promo tool cuts YouTube walkthroughs and TikTok ads from real screens; ten apps have videos.

Flamingo Map got its App Store listing and a live landing. Cyllene's reply drafter falls back to Claude and holds back answers only I can give. The Flamingo reply is drafted; the recording is on take three. Nothing has gone to Apple; Cyllene's iPhone app is live.

Product Hunt ate the rest of the day: I fixed old names and a blanked price across 32 products. Fourteen apps got launch kits.

The portfolio site's prices didn't match the store, so I rebuilt them from App Store Connect. Seven apps it listed at 99 cents are free; Epiphany is now 99 cents like Echo and Kernel. The three paid apps have no in-app purchase yet, so they go free with a 99 cent premium unlock until that build is approved.

Turing shipped version 10: a small picker in plain C inside my Joshua Tree kernel, right on 81 percent of test rows. A coder trained on my own code runs in the app; its first answer had a bug, so it's not trusted yet. Her coder joins the Mac download next; the goal is 28 of 40 sealed coding tasks. OEIS asked for one fix; I made it and sent in another. Noema got a Mac and iPhone app and a field chart on its landing.

Kernel books with no cover get drawn initials. The iPhone and Mac apps are next.

I went through about 160 saved X bookmarks and picked the next plays: a product simulator, a small Claude Code model (on hold for disk), voice leads for Echo, and Kernel covers.

I set up a Chrome extension that reloads YouTube when an ad plays. Its code makes no network calls, but one fallback led to a lookalike domain, so I cut it from my copy. None of three test videos served an ad, so the reload is still untested. It looks good to me.

Sales only go back to June, and few have earned anything, so the rest go on the cut list, starting with the lookalikes Apple rejected. Next: the app audit, then Kernel's cover fallback.

I gave about 32 landing pages an FAQ section, written so search engines can show the answers under the link. Nine apps still wait on a build or Pages step.
