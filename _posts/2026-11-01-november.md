## Nov 1

Accessibility took the day: 98 pages pass an automated check, and twenty apps got fixes. Sighting and Co-Stanza went to App Review; Apple wants every post reportable and blockable. Joshua Tree ran full screen on a real Raspberry Pi 4 for the first time. Board testing found five bugs, all fixed that afternoon, and release eight passed with Bluetooth.

## Nov 2

Duke ran Papers, Please overnight; it reached day 14 and learned to defuse the bomb.

Joshemon, my Game Boy game, went public, then got six releases.

Joshua Tree shipped Shortcuts, Bluetooth and YouTube; its Pi graphics chip runs on a driver I wrote by hand. Keyrate got its App Store record, free in 175 countries and collecting nothing.

## Nov 6

I filed 31 Apple Notes into roadmaps and the vault, added eleven Kernel chapters from four books, and deleted 184 source photos; 566 still wait.

Apple rejected Keyrate as spam, matching its word list to two typing tests. I rebuilt the list from public domain novels; both are back in review.

## Nov 7

Cyllene is back before Apple with a reviewer key and demo video; twelve apps wait. Nine projects broke under Xcode 27; all are fixed.

Apple rejected Elah, Co-Stanza and Sighting as spam lookalikes. App-specific replies to all six threads got Horula and Atlin through.

After comparing ten rival browsers, I added downloads, site icons, a fire button, HTTPS first and translation. Mac is in review, iPhone waits.

## Nov 8

iPhone and Mac builds are back in review. Plaintxt's builds wait; Apple wants bigger iOS changes or I drop iOS.

Cyllene's onboarding has four real screens; real login ships after iPhone review.

## Nov 9

The iPhone and Mac update is in review; the web is live. Joshua Tree's next build merged, closing 32 issues; PC ping is open.

Epiphany's iPhone statement upload never worked, so my October statement missed the graph; the fix is untested.

## Nov 10

Hamurapi's dark landing shows the game at night; its trailer is approved on both platforms.

Apple rejected Patois for not signing in with Apple, which was never spam. The sheet stayed open after sign-in, so it looked dead. Fixed; the iPhone build is back in review.

## Nov 11

Typed math answers count in any order, and math courses show x², √2 and π. Pre-Calc 11 went from ten to sixty questions; Pre-Calc 12 explains every answer.

I built a Things page for jaybulb.com: 55 things I use. I also fixed Patois still saying Tonchi in password reset emails.

## Nov 13

Apple locked my developer account until I accepted a new agreement. Tocks was rejected for background audio, since sleep sounds play on a locked phone. Apple asked what Delete account does; lost-pet posts now go too. Co-Stanza is live on iPhone and Mac.

## Nov 14

Sent an iPhone update to Apple. The one-word Nimble name belongs to another developer.

## Nov 15

I spent the morning making Nimble faster.

Joshua Tree's dimming and saver are merged. The Pi's auto lock is in review: only my password opens it. Network printing is written.

I built the three pieces Doorstock's spec was missing: spring costs, a label scanner and a monthly accountant CSV. Mac is built; iPhone waits on the appeal.

Talli got a money log with a yearly limit bar for the tax export. The web and server are live; iPhone and Mac need TestFlight.

Kernel now imports a PDF or EPUB you own on every device and summarizes each chapter. Then the library came up empty; I recovered most summaries from an old Mac cache.

Epiphany is free on the web now; Pro is $1.99 a month or $14.99 a year, and anyone who paid $1 keeps it for life. I shut three billing holes, testing each live; the iPhone paywall is next. I killed Gato and dropped the CanLII app; Temida does that search.

## Nov 16

Board night ran past midnight on a stale card with the wrong clock, so every secure download failed. Paddle froze because every frame repainted the whole screen; now it paints only what moved, with a ball trail and a big score. The card still needs one reflash. I also started a carrier board for the Compute Module 5.

Joshua Tree's version 14 was meant for printing and scanning, but I have no printer, so it shipped as graphics acceleration; its first job cleared a test tile on the board. The Mac can now drive the Pi and see its screen. Blue default and graphics commands are in review. I also started the 64-bit PC port, the road to 15; a 64-bit kernel boots in the emulator, not pushed yet. Scans ran the Pi out of memory; each job now frees its own. Wi-Fi stopped dropping packets.

Samantha writes captions by listening now, using Whisper when a video has no subtitle file; the first real test came out word for word. Her trailer is done: thirty seconds, narrated. Her training runs without me; a math overflow zeroed the first round, so it now uses safer precision. The LaCie drive is gone, so the 9B model and that data are lost until I get a new drive.

Then I cleared the backlog. The notes routine now reads every account and deletes a note only after filing it. All 58 Apple Notes went into the roadmaps of 26 projects, private bits into my vault, and Notes is empty now.

## Nov 17

Keyrate moved into its own repo, with a new icon and a drill mode that builds practice from the keys I miss most. An Apple outage blocked a stale draft in the iPhone review thread. Both Keyrate builds still wait for review from Nov 6; nothing was rejected.

Samantha's video tools are finished: compress a clip, grab a still frame, lay a song under a video. The landing opens on her trailer full-screen behind the headline, and scrolling no longer kills the live demo on iPhone. I cut her docs from 21 files to 7. The trailer also shows her booting Joshua Tree.

Her fixed training round scored 97.5% on its own check, trained overnight on GitHub with no Mac involved.

I tried local models for years. Claude Code on the 4B one still can't debug a config; it only writes small scripts, and auto mode can't run on it. The 9B needs 15 GB of disk I don't have.

## Nov 18

Joshua Tree's landing lost its typewriter headline for good; a check now fails the build if it ever comes back. The Spaces and Mission Control clips were refilmed with a card attached. The app wall is short real clips of each app, filmed in an emulator, with the stills kept as posters. A new desktop section shows Spaces, Mission Control, Spotlight and Launchpad; Update, Bluetooth and Samantha building an app have clips too. Next is the Pi drawing its desktop, once Joshua tests it on the board.

Turing's tool picker reached its major release overnight. Fresh test rows passed all three bars, and a new guard refuses about half as many right answers as before.
