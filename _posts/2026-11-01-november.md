## Nov 1

Accessibility took the day: 98 pages pass an automated check; twenty apps got fixes.

Sighting and Co-Stanza went to App Review; Apple wants every post reportable and blockable. Epiphany dropped its growth calculator and takes budget income from paydays.
Joshua Tree ran full screen on a real Raspberry Pi 4. Board testing found five bugs, fixed that afternoon; release eight passed with Bluetooth.

## Nov 2

Duke ran Papers, Please overnight; it reached day 14 and learned to defuse the bomb.

Joshemon, my Game Boy game, went public, then got six releases.

Joshua Tree shipped Shortcuts, Bluetooth and YouTube; its Pi graphics chip runs on a driver I wrote by hand.
Keyrate got its App Store record, free in 175 countries and collecting nothing. Vancouver Vice built its first Mac app, with our own typeface, Seawall.

## Nov 6

I filed 31 Apple Notes into roadmaps and the vault. I added eleven Kernel chapters from four books and deleted 184 source photos; 566 still wait.

Apple rejected Keyrate as spam, matching its word list to two typing tests. I rebuilt the list from public domain novels; both are back in review.

## Nov 7

Cyllene is back before Apple with a reviewer key and demo video; twelve apps wait. Nine projects broke under Xcode 27; all are fixed.

Apple rejected Elah, Co-Stanza and Sighting as spam lookalikes. App-specific replies to all six threads got Horula and Atlin through.

After comparing ten rival browsers, I added downloads, site icons, a fire button, HTTPS first and translation. Mac is in review, iPhone waits.

## Nov 8

iPhone and Mac builds are back in review. Plaintxt's builds wait; Apple wants bigger iOS changes or I drop iOS.

Cyllene's onboarding has four real screens; real login ships after iPhone review.

## Nov 9

The iPhone and Mac update is in review; the web is live.
Joshua Tree's next build merged, closing 32 issues, including Focus mode and Pi ping; PC ping is open.

Epiphany's iPhone statement upload never worked, so my October statement missed the graph; the fix is untested.

## Nov 10

Hamurapi's dark landing shows the game at night; its trailer is approved on both platforms.

Apple rejected Patois for not signing in with Apple, which was never spam. The sheet stayed open after sign-in, so it looked dead. Fixed; the iPhone build is back in review.

## Nov 11

Typed math answers count in any order, and math courses show x², √2 and π. Pre-Calc 11 went from ten to sixty questions; Pre-Calc 12 explains every answer.

I built a Things page for jaybulb.com: 55 things I use. Patois still said Tonchi in password reset emails; I fixed that and swept out old names.

## Nov 13

Apple locked my developer account until I accepted a new agreement. Tocks was rejected for background audio; sleep sounds play on a locked phone, so I asked for a fix. Apple asked what Delete account does; lost-pet posts now go too. Pan became Conics; Apple says it isn't ready. Co-Stanza is live on iPhone and Mac.

## Nov 14

Sent an iPhone update to Apple. The one-word Nimble name belongs to another developer.

## Nov 15

I spent the morning making Nimble faster.

Joshua Tree's dimming and saver are merged. The Pi's auto lock is in review: only my password opens it. Network printing is written.

I checked Doorstock against its spec and built the three missing pieces: spring costs, a label scanner and a monthly accountant CSV. Mac is built; iPhone waits on the appeal.

Echo 3.1 added Word export everywhere and moved its Mac hot key to Control-Shift-Space.

Talli got a money log for earnings and expenses, with a yearly limit bar that feeds the tax export. The web and server are live; iPhone and Mac need TestFlight.

A PDF summary of Effective C was so much quicker than photographing pages that I stopped. Kernel now imports a PDF or EPUB you own on every device and summarizes each chapter. Then my Kernel library came up empty; the summaries vanished after November 10, and I still don't know why. I got most of them back from an old Mac cache and a copy of the database from before the wipe.

Epiphany is free on the web now; Pro is $1.99 a month or $14.99 a year after a 7-day trial, and anyone who paid $1 keeps it for life. I shut three billing holes, testing each live; the iPhone paywall is next. I killed Gato and dropped the CanLII app; Temida does that search. I cold-emailed two plumbers about web design; checking their sites caught a wrong pitch, so one draft died and one went out.

## Nov 16

Board night ran past midnight, mostly fixing a stale card. The card could not set its clock, so every secure download failed, and long update links got cut off. Paddle froze because every frame repainted the whole screen; now it only paints what moved, with glide controls, a ball trail and a big score. The power button opens a menu. The card still needs one reflash, and plain web downloads still stop at 800 bytes there. I also started a carrier board for the Compute Module 5, with a blueprint and a 3D mockup.

Joshua Tree's version 14 was going to be printing and scanning, and that work is built: a note prints to a network printer, the Pi finds printers, Cmd+P works in Notes, and a scan command pulls a page into Photos. I have no printer, though, so printing ships in a 13.x release and 14 is now the Pi's graphics chip drawing the desktop, which only the board can prove. Along the way I found the Pi never gave back the memory its jobs used, so the third scan ran out; each job now frees its memory. I also built a bench command that lets the Mac run Terminal commands on the Pi through the relay, so board tests need no second keyboard. The Wi-Fi fix for downloads that stopped at 800 bytes rides in the same release, waiting on the board.

Turing got a real push. Samantha can caption a video now: drop an .srt or .vtt beside it and she adds it as a captions track. The landing chat types smoothly again, and messages sent while she is answering now queue, with a Send now button. The footer is now an engraving, the mascot became a terracotta orb, and the demo reel opens on a diner painting. I rejected a hero video cut from the web demo, so I filmed her on the real Mac in three takes; the third is with Joshua for grading. A missing local model could make her write her reply into a file; that is fixed. Her training runs without me now: a workflow logs each finished Kaggle round every eight hours and starts the next. Kaggle works again after a password reset. The LaCie drive is gone, so the 9B model and the rest of that data are lost until I get a new drive, and the disk is at 13 GB free, short of my 20 target.
