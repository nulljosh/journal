## Nov 1

Accessibility took the day: 98 pages pass an automated check, and twenty apps got fixes. Sighting and Co-Stanza went to App Review; Apple wants every post reportable and blockable. Joshua Tree ran full screen on a real Raspberry Pi 4 for the first time. Board testing found five bugs, all fixed that afternoon; release eight passed with Bluetooth.

## Nov 2

Duke ran Papers, Please overnight; it reached day 14 and learned to defuse the bomb.

Joshemon, my Game Boy game, went public, then got six releases.

Joshua Tree shipped Shortcuts, Bluetooth and YouTube; its Pi graphics chip runs on a driver I wrote by hand. Keyrate got its App Store record, free in 175 countries and collecting nothing.

## Nov 6

I filed 31 Apple Notes into roadmaps and the vault, added eleven Kernel chapters from four books, and deleted 184 source photos; 566 wait.

Apple rejected Keyrate as spam, matching its word list to two typing tests. I rebuilt the list from public domain novels; both are back in review.

## Nov 7

Cyllene is back before Apple with a reviewer key and demo video; twelve apps wait. Nine projects broke under Xcode 27; all are fixed.

Apple rejected Elah, Co-Stanza and Sighting as spam lookalikes. App-specific replies to all six threads got Horula and Atlin through.

After comparing ten rival browsers, I added downloads, site icons, a fire button, HTTPS first and translation. Mac is in review, iPhone waits.

## Nov 8

iPhone and Mac builds are back in review. Plaintxt's builds wait; Apple wants bigger iOS changes or I drop iOS.

Cyllene's onboarding has four real screens; real login ships after iPhone review.

## Nov 9

The iPhone and Mac update is in review; the web is live. Joshua Tree's next build merged, closing 32 issues; PC ping is open.

Epiphany's iPhone statement upload never worked, so October missed the graph; the fix is untested.

## Nov 10

Hamurapi's trailer is approved everywhere.

Apple rejected Patois for not signing in with Apple, which was never spam. The sheet stayed open after sign-in, so it looked dead. Fixed; the iPhone build is back in review.

## Nov 11

Typed math answers count in any order, and math courses show x², √2 and π. Pre-Calc 11 went from ten to sixty questions; Pre-Calc 12 explains every answer.

I built a Things page for jaybulb.com: 55 things I use.
## Nov 13

Apple locked my developer account until I accepted a new agreement. Tocks was rejected for background audio. Apple asked what Delete account does; lost-pet posts now go too. Co-Stanza is live on iPhone and Mac.

## Nov 14

Sent an iPhone update to Apple. The one-word Nimble name belongs to another developer.

## Nov 15

Joshua Tree's dimming and saver are merged. The Pi's auto lock is in review: only my password opens it.

I built the three pieces Doorstock's spec was missing: spring costs, a label scanner and a monthly accountant CSV. Mac is built; iPhone waits on the appeal.

Talli got a money log with a yearly limit bar for the tax export. The web and server are live; iPhone and Mac need TestFlight.

Kernel now imports a PDF or EPUB you own on every device and summarizes each chapter. Then the library came up empty; I recovered most summaries from an old cache.

Epiphany is free on the web now; Pro is $1.99 a month or $14.99 a year, and anyone who paid $1 keeps it for life. I shut three billing holes, testing each live; the iPhone paywall is next. I killed Gato and dropped the CanLII app; Temida does that search.

## Nov 16

Board night ran past midnight on a stale card with the wrong clock, so every secure download failed. Paddle froze because every frame repainted the whole screen; now it paints only what moved. The card still needs one reflash. I also started a carrier board for the Compute Module 5.

Joshua Tree's version 14 was meant for printing and scanning, but I have no printer, so it shipped as graphics acceleration; its first job cleared a test tile. The Mac now drives the Pi and sees its screen. Blue default and graphics commands are in review. I also started the 64-bit PC port; its kernel boots in the emulator, unpushed.
Samantha writes captions by listening; the first test came out word for word. Her trailer is done: thirty seconds, narrated. Her training runs without me; a math overflow zeroed round one, so it uses safer precision. The LaCie is gone, taking the 9B model's data with it.

The notes routine now deletes a note only after filing it. All 58 Apple Notes went into the roadmaps of 26 projects, private bits into my vault; Notes is empty.

## Nov 17

Keyrate got a new icon and a drill mode built from the keys I miss most. Both Keyrate builds still wait for review from Nov 6; nothing was rejected.

Samantha's video tools are finished: compress a clip, grab a still, lay a song under a video. The landing opens on her trailer full-screen behind the headline, and scrolling no longer kills the live iPhone demo.

Her fixed round scored 97.5% on its own check, trained overnight on GitHub.

The 4B still can't debug a config or run auto mode; the 9B needs 15 GB I don't have.

## Nov 18

The landing lost its typewriter headline for good; a build check now blocks it. A new desktop section shows Spaces, Mission Control, Spotlight and Launchpad. Next is the Pi drawing its desktop, once Joshua tests it.
I graded the Joshua Tree landing a B-: good content, muddled order, too many repeats. I cut the repeats and made the desktop clips one swipeable strip. Since it went live by hand I added a top bar for each section and filled the app wall, with Depths as the big tile.
Turing's picker reached its major release. A fresh sealed set of 420 rows passed all three bars, and a new guard refuses about half as many right answers. It is on Hugging Face; both sealed sets are spent.

I tidied the code folder, deleting a duplicate skill copy and 21 old worktrees, freeing 2.5 GB. I also repaired my Claude skills, mangled by a Codex find-and-replace; all 29 now link to one shared folder, and duplicate commands are merged or archived.

Vancouver Vice's Unreal project lived only on the LaCie and was never in git, so the city and builds are gone. The web game and map data survived on GitHub; I'm rebuilding from those scripts.

## Nov 21

Airwell passed App Review and went live on the App Store.

Nimble's landing got rebuilt around one big stage: type a math or unit question and the answer lands huge behind the headline. The dark middle of the page got big yellow numbers and a yellow band, and the footer scene moves while it is on screen. The API had two bugs too: a cancel message got an error back, and error answers were kept for five minutes. Both are fixed and the tests pass.
