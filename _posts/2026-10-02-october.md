## Oct 1 to 3

Joshua Tree's second release sandboxed twenty-six apps; Bookrank works offline on iPhone. Auto-trading is live.

## Oct 4

My idea app became Hikko; iPhone and Mac are in review. Lexly became Tonchi, now 165 courses.

## Oct 5 to 8

Apple rejected seven apps as spam, and Hikko over screenshots.

The Pi 4 booted Joshua Tree at 1080p once I wrote the Wi-Fi login its chip lacked.

## Oct 11 to 13

I built a digest of twenty AI accounts on X; Plank can call models. Joshua Tree has a landing.

## Oct 16 to 18

Talli's Stripe webhook rejected purchases, fixed; jaybulb.com mail works at every address. All 41 apps got a new brand kit and icon.

A security header blocked my shared tokens file, so pages fell back to Times New Roman, and I fixed ten repos. Mailbag and Patois went into review, and I cut GitHub from 62 repos to 31 and erased my plan folder from public history.

## Oct 19 to 21

Notate's offline update is live on iPhone and Watch, and it became Echo. Glyphbox became Icelus, Temida's Apple sign-in fix cleared, Intake was taken so it's Machaon, and Curvely went into review as Pan. The calling app became Oohaah after three names.

Temida is my litigation app, Thinkpond became Melete, and Hermes, a Helm clone for App Store Connect, sends nothing without my yes. Nimble got A+ on every BC grade 10 to 12 math, science and socials exam, and wrong word problems fell from 46 to 2. NYC Survive is Gastown now: a crew, a Steam Clock and self-running drills, no raids or cash.

## Oct 22 to 25

Epiphany splits subscriptions by currency, so US plans stop hitting my Canadian total.
Coranto became Bugle, but Apple refused that, so the store says BugleNews. Clock became Horula, and five Apple appeals won.

## Oct 26 to 27

Duke beat my desert platformer Mirage in 3,725 steps, and I booked eight Product Hunt launches for Oct 28 to Nov 6 and appealed Cartons' spam rejection. Kaggle suspended my account, so training waits on appeal. Curbfind became Scrounger, since Apple refused Scrounge.

Noema's biggest day: a Lean proof showed Busy Beaver 2,3 is 38, and ClinicalTrials.gov shows 407 impossible standard deviations in 52 trials.

## Oct 28 to 29

Apple cleared my Sidewise spam answers, so I pulled it into Bugle, whose iPhone and Mac builds are live. Wordroot became Eponym, and Homeward became Sighting. Kernel went into App Review, then added a sleep timer, three narration languages, Goodreads sign-in and AI-free quizzes.

Joshua Tree got a launch film and Plank calls C. Millie, my phone assistant, shipped ten releases with spam blocking and caller memory, catching three bugs by calling her own number. I split my labs into ten public repos, with GitHub's limit holding four back, and turned Keyrate into four versions sharing thirty tests.

## Oct 30

Kernel's release is on GitHub, with chapters 1 to 4 from phone photos; open issues went from 43 to zero. I fixed the Noema links the labs split broke.

I renamed the Bible app Elah, since Apple had the plain name. I deleted six dead GitHub repos and set a rule: a project gets a repo once it has a store listing or domain. I picked eight unlaunched apps for Product Hunt, weekly from Nov 13. I tightened the rules: big jumps wait on me, features are minors, fixes are patches, shipped numbers never change.

Temida's money bars stopped animating, every headline uses one font, and Airwell went public.

## Oct 31

Millie quotes my status and texts back, Elah got reading plans on Today, and I fixed the iPad button. I filed thirteen Apple Notes into roadmaps and the wiki, then emptied Notes.
Every open rejection got an answer or new build. Patois signs in through my server, fixing Apple's Sign in with Apple error. Airwell's new build says Airwell, not Windgate. Scrounger went back, and Oohaah, Cartons, Horula and Atlin still wait. Hermes is Cyllene now, and its iPhone app got App Lock, sales at a glance and a launch film, all waiting for review.

Elah's first iPhone build was rejected, and Apple sign-in is throttled. I cut the NIV and put nine public domain Bibles in as real text, offline, with a picker in app and web. The headline is now Read the Bible, anywhere; both wait on review.

## Nov 1

Spent the day on accessibility: 60 landing and 38 web app pages pass an automated check, and about twenty apps got fixes. Nimble got Reduce Motion guards and bigger tab labels, and its new accessibility audit flags 13 contrast and text size problems I still have to fix.
Keyrate got desktop, iPad and Homebrew apps and a Test menu. Noema's big proofs now build one at a time, since CI ran out of memory.

Sighting, the lost and found pets app, went to the App Store. Apple requires public posts to be reportable and blockable, so each can be reported, hidden or blocked. The desktop and Android apps can post now too, with a photo and an edit link, and they moved to the same backend.

Co-Stanza reached the App Store on the same poems as the web, with author pages, search, saved poems, sharing and editing your own.
Epiphany dropped the growth calculator and skips transfers in review, and its search got a pass on iPhone, Mac and web. Budget income now comes from statement paydays, and debts count down each month. Talli lost its dead session store, and its Chinese and Punjabi went back out, since benefit wording needs a human translator. Coda's store listing now reads in twenty more languages, and Gastown's settings work: saves refresh, the sound switch flips, and auto-save sticks. Atlin's REW and Zillow feeds are both live, with REW's real photos kept. Nimble got a Linux package, two widgets and offline steps for equations and derivatives.

Joshua Tree ran full screen on a real Raspberry Pi 4 for the first time, sharp. The first board pass found five bugs, all fixed that afternoon, and Wi-Fi, Settings, the debugger and building on the Pi work. The screen stays awake with no keyboard or mouse, and the Pi shows live stock prices and weather with my own watchlist. Bluetooth pairing is built but not pushed.

Nearly every landing now runs the real product behind the hero, from Nimble answering its own questions to Duke playing real game frames.
Vancouver Vice finally has me in it: a photo of me became a rigged 3D model in my crewneck and glasses. The city got glass towers, crosswalks, real cars and shops you walk into. The heist opens inside the Georgia store, where you grab six Mac minis while staff in blue shirts chase you out. Between jobs you roam free with yellow markers for the next one, and new missions with a fictional McDonald's manager on Granville end with you tailing his gold car. A third walk-in room, a garage in Kits, has a sectional door, a workbench and a white work truck.
