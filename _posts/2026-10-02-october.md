## Oct 1-3

Joshua Tree's second release sandboxed twenty-six apps; Bookrank works offline on iPhone. 

## Oct 4

My idea app became Hikko; iPhone and Mac are in review. Lexly became Tonchi, now 165 courses.

## Oct 5-8

Apple rejected seven apps as spam, and Hikko over screenshots.

The Pi 4 booted Joshua Tree at 1080p once I wrote the Wi-Fi login its chip lacked.

## Oct 11-13

I built a digest of twenty AI accounts on X; Plank can call models. Joshua Tree has a landing.

## Oct 16-18

Talli's Stripe webhook rejected purchases, fixed; jaybulb.com mail works at every address. All 41 apps got a new brand kit and icon.

A security header blocked my shared tokens file, so pages fell back to Times New Roman; I fixed ten repos. Mailbag and Patois went into review, I cut GitHub from 62 repos to 31, and erased my plan folder from public history.

## Oct 19-21

Notate's offline update is live on iPhone and Watch; it became Echo. Glyphbox became Icelus, Temida's Apple sign-in fix cleared, Intake was taken so it's Machaon, and Curvely went into review as Pan. The calling app became Oohaah after three names.

Thinkpond became Melete, and Hermes sends nothing without my yes. Nimble got A+ on every BC grade 10 to 12 exam, and wrong word problems fell from 46 to 2. NYC Survive is Gastown now: a crew, a Steam Clock, no raids or cash.

## Oct 22-25

Epiphany splits subscriptions by currency, so US plans stop hitting my Canadian total. Coranto became Bugle, but Apple refused that, so the store says BugleNews. Clock became Horula, and five Apple appeals won.

## Oct 26-27

Duke beat my desert platformer Mirage in 3,725 steps, and I booked eight Product Hunt launches for Oct 28 to Nov 6 and appealed Cartons' spam rejection. Curbfind became Scrounger, since Apple refused Scrounge.

Noema's big day: a Lean proof showed Busy Beaver 2,3 is 38, and ClinicalTrials.gov shows 407 impossible standard deviations.

## Oct 28-29

Apple cleared my Sidewise spam answers, so I pulled it into Bugle, live on iPhone and Mac. Wordroot became Eponym, Homeward became Sighting. Kernel went into App Review with a sleep timer, three narration languages and AI-free quizzes.

Joshua Tree got a launch film and Plank calls C. Millie, my phone assistant, shipped ten releases with spam blocking and caller memory, catching three bugs by calling herself. I split my labs into ten public repos and made Keyrate four versions sharing thirty tests.

## Oct 30

Kernel's release is on GitHub, with chapters 1 to 4 from phone photos; open issues went from 43 to zero. I fixed the Noema links the labs split broke.

I deleted six dead GitHub repos and set a rule: a project gets a repo once it has a store listing or domain. I picked eight apps for Product Hunt, weekly from Nov 13, and made big version jumps wait on me.

Airwell went public.

## Oct 31

Elah got reading plans on Today, and I fixed the iPad button. I filed thirteen Apple Notes into roadmaps and the wiki, then emptied Notes. Patois signs in through my server, fixing Apple's error. Airwell's new build says Airwell, not Windgate. Scrounger went back, and Oohaah, Cartons, Horula and Atlin still wait. Hermes is Cyllene now; its iPhone app waits for review.

Elah's first iPhone build was rejected, and Apple sign-in is throttled. I cut the NIV and added nine public domain Bibles as real offline text, with a picker in app and web. The headline is now Read the Bible, anywhere; both wait on review.

## Nov 1

Accessibility took the day: 98 pages pass an automated check, and about twenty apps got fixes. Nimble got Reduce Motion, a Linux package and two widgets. Keyrate went desktop, iPad and Homebrew; Noema's proofs now build one at a time, since CI ran out of memory.

Sighting, the lost and found pets app, went to the App Store. Apple wants every post reportable and blockable: each can be reported, hidden or blocked. Co-Stanza reached the App Store with the web's poems and search.

Epiphany dropped the growth calculator, skips transfers in review, got a search pass, and takes budget income from paydays. Coda's listing reads in twenty more languages, and Atlin's REW and Zillow feeds are live.

I ran Joshua Tree full screen on a real Raspberry Pi 4 for the first time, and it was sharp. Board testing found five bugs, all fixed that afternoon; the eighth release passed with Bluetooth in Settings, a painted tree mark and an Apple-style boot. The Pi stays awake with no keyboard or mouse, showing live stock prices and weather.

Gaps against Berkeley's OS course became the Berkeley list: threads with locks, all four Pi cores, pipes, apps that wait on other apps, sockets, and ten-millisecond slices so a busy app can't freeze the desktop. That release is in its last CI run; the next, with USB sticks, is ready on my Mac. The window and dock lag is fixed: three of four cores were spinning on one shared line.

The wallpaper is a dithered pixel-art Joshua Tree park at dusk.

Nearly every landing runs the real product behind its hero, and I rigged my Vancouver Vice character from my photo. Tonight he got real moves: running, jumping, and a death fall when you get wasted. The North Shore rises over the city and BC Place has its red band. The big one is Brookswood, rebuilt on real streets with Mount Baker on the horizon. The game is Apache 2.0 now and live.

## Nov 2

I let Duke run Papers, Please overnight. Days 11-14 went through, but day 15 kept failing: the family died once, a debt got me a game over, and the bomb went off twice. The bot learned to defuse the bomb, hand the EZIC messenger his papers, and let inspect mode check height and weight. Then the Mac's built-in text reader broke machine-wide; restarting its pieces didn't help, so day 15 waits on a reboot.

Joshémon, my Game Boy monster game, went public with eight gyms and a league.

Joshua Tree went from its tenth release to its thirteenth today, adding Shortcuts, Bluetooth and YouTube.

Keyrate got its App Store record today: free in 175 countries, with a privacy label saying nothing is collected. The icon is now a staggered keyboard, since the old outline keys read as 888, and light mode is white everywhere. The iPhone, iPad and Mac builds go in now.

I wrote my own summaries of Kernel's last 88 book photos, after an old owner ID failed every upload. I cut two duplicate chapters and listed the books still needing my photos.

Vancouver Vice built its first packaged Mac app. The title's Enter key, save slots and tutorial box passed QA, and a punch no longer freezes me. I gave it our own typeface, Seawall. The Lower Mainland runs at 60 frames a second with cops who chase you, but it still feels laggy and the art is so-so. Next: a mission inside a store, guns, more cops as stars rise, and car damage.
