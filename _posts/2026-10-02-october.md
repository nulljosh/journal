## Oct 1 to 3

Joshua Tree's second release sandboxed twenty-six apps; Bookrank works offline on iPhone. Auto-trading is live.

## Oct 4

My idea app became Hikko; iPhone and Mac are in review. Lexly became Tonchi, now 165 courses.

## Oct 5 to 8

Apple rejected seven apps as spam, and Hikko over screenshots.

The Pi 4 booted Joshua Tree at 1080p once I wrote the Wi-Fi login its chip lacked.

## Oct 11 to 13

I built a digest of twenty AI accounts on X; Plank can call models. Joshua Tree has a landing.

## Oct 16 to 18

Talli's Stripe webhook rejected purchases, fixed; jaybulb.com mail works at every address. All 41 apps got a new brand kit and icon.

A security header blocked my shared tokens file, so pages fell back to Times New Roman, and I fixed ten repos. Mailbag and Patois went into review, and I cut GitHub from 62 repos to 31 and erased my plan folder from public history.

## Oct 19 to 21

Notate's offline update is live on iPhone and Watch, and it became Echo. Glyphbox became Icelus, Temida's Apple sign-in fix cleared, Intake was taken so it's Machaon, and Curvely went into review as Pan. The calling app became Oohaah after three names.

Temida is my litigation app, Thinkpond became Melete, and Hermes, a Helm clone for App Store Connect, sends nothing without my yes. Nimble got A+ on every BC grade 10 to 12 math, science and socials exam, and wrong word problems fell from 46 to 2. NYC Survive is Gastown now: a crew, a Steam Clock and self-running drills, no raids or cash.

## Oct 22 to 25

Epiphany splits subscriptions by currency, so US plans stop hitting my Canadian total.
Coranto became Bugle, but Apple refused that, so the store says BugleNews. Clock became Horula, and five Apple appeals won.

## Oct 26 to 27

Duke beat my desert platformer Mirage in 3,725 steps, and I booked eight Product Hunt launches for Oct 28 to Nov 6 and appealed Cartons' spam rejection. Kaggle suspended my account, so training waits on appeal. Curbfind became Scrounger, since Apple refused Scrounge.

Noema's biggest day: a Lean proof showed Busy Beaver 2,3 is 38, and ClinicalTrials.gov shows 407 impossible standard deviations in 52 trials.

## Oct 28 to 29

Apple cleared my Sidewise spam answers, so I pulled it into Bugle, whose iPhone and Mac builds are live. Wordroot became Eponym, and Homeward became Sighting. Kernel went into App Review with a sleep timer, three narration languages and AI-free quizzes.

Joshua Tree got a launch film and Plank calls C. Millie, my phone assistant, shipped ten releases with spam blocking and caller memory, catching three bugs by calling her own number. I split my labs into ten public repos, and turned Keyrate into four versions sharing thirty tests.

## Oct 30

Kernel's release is on GitHub, with chapters 1 to 4 from phone photos; open issues went from 43 to zero. I fixed the Noema links the labs split broke.

I renamed the Bible app Elah, since Apple had the plain name. I deleted six dead GitHub repos and set a rule: a project gets a repo once it has a store listing or domain. I picked eight unlaunched apps for Product Hunt, weekly from Nov 13. I tightened the versioning rules, so big jumps wait on me.

Temida's money bars stopped animating, headlines share one font, and Airwell went public.

## Oct 31

Millie quotes my status and texts back, Elah got reading plans on Today, and I fixed the iPad button. I filed thirteen Apple Notes into roadmaps and the wiki, then emptied Notes.
Patois signs in through my server, which fixed Apple's error. Airwell's new build says Airwell, not Windgate. Scrounger went back, and Oohaah, Cartons, Horula and Atlin still wait. Hermes is Cyllene now, and its iPhone app, with App Lock and a launch film, waits for review.

Elah's first iPhone build was rejected, and Apple sign-in is throttled. I cut the NIV and put nine public domain Bibles in as real text, offline, with a picker in app and web. The headline is now Read the Bible, anywhere; both wait on review.

## Nov 1

## Nov 1

Spent the day on accessibility: 60 landing and 38 web app pages pass an automated check, and about twenty apps got fixes. Nimble got Reduce Motion guards, bigger tab labels, a Linux package and two widgets, though its audit flags 13 contrast and size problems. Keyrate got desktop, iPad and Homebrew apps. Noema's big proofs now build one at a time, since CI ran out of memory.

Sighting, the lost and found pets app, went to the App Store. Apple wants public posts reportable and blockable, so each can be reported, hidden or blocked, and desktop and Android can post now too. Co-Stanza reached the App Store with the web's poems, plus author pages, search and sharing.

Epiphany dropped the growth calculator, skips transfers in review and got a search pass, and budget income now comes from statement paydays. Talli's Chinese and Punjabi went back out, since benefit wording needs a human translator. Coda's store listing reads in twenty more languages, and Atlin's REW and Zillow feeds are live.

Joshua Tree was the big one. I ran it full screen on a real Raspberry Pi 4 for the first time, and it was sharp. The first board pass found five bugs, all fixed that afternoon, and the eighth release shipped and passed the board test: Bluetooth lives in Settings, the mark is a painted tree, and the boot is Apple-style. A pink speckle was a loose HDMI plug. The Pi stays awake with no keyboard or mouse and shows live stock prices and weather from my watchlist.

The gaps against Berkeley's operating systems course became the Berkeley list: threads with locks, all four Pi cores, pipes, apps that start and wait on other apps, sockets, and ten-millisecond slices so a busy app can't freeze the desktop. That release is in its last CI run. The next one is ready on my Mac, unpushed, with USB sticks, memory that loads on first touch and a block cache. The window and dock lag is fixed: three of four cores were spinning and bouncing one shared line of memory. The song pop at start and pause is gone, the arrow shows at boot, and dock zoom is off by default.

The wallpaper is a dithered pixel-art Joshua Tree park at dusk, which Joshua says looks like the Sable album cover. The dock line reads "Great artists steal. Picasso." The restart crackle comes from the Pi's own boot firmware, not my system; a muted test proved it, and next I'll try a restart that skips the firmware. I drew the next version's target screens with real fonts, including a Software Update with a fallback kernel, and Burrow goes back to Files. The disk is at 12 GB free.

Nearly every landing runs the real product behind its hero. Vancouver Vice finally has me in it, as a rigged 3D model made from a photo in my crewneck and glasses.
